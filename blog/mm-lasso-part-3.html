Part 3: Auxiliary KKT Conditions

MM Algorithms and the Lasso · Anant Mathur · July 2026

1 · MM Algorithms 2 · The Lasso 3 · Auxiliary KKT 4 · An Interesting Bound 5 · Water-Filling Demo

One scalar per group

Part 2 left us with the group-lasso KKT conditions in $\bb{R}^p$. The central move of the paper is a change of variables: introduce auxiliary variables $\v\gamma = [\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_J]^\top \in \bb{R}_+^J$ — one non-negative scalar per group — and define

$$ \m G(\v\gamma) := \Big(\m I + \sum_{j=1}^{J} \gamma_j \m Z_j \m Z_j^\top\Big)^{-1}. $$

Readers familiar with ridge regression or Gaussian processes will recognise $\m G(\v\gamma)$ as the inverse of a regularised Gram matrix: each $\gamma_j$ acts like a per-group precision. The optimization moves from a space of dimension $p$ to one of dimension $J$.

The equivalence theorem

Theorem (Auxiliary Variable KKT Conditions). Let $\v\theta_j = \gamma_j \m Z_j^\top \m G(\v\gamma)\, \v y$ for $j = 1, \ldots, J$. Then $\v\theta$ satisfies the group-lasso KKT conditions of Part 2 if and only if $\v\gamma$ satisfies

$$ \begin{aligned} \big\|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\v\gamma)\, \v y\big\|_2 &= \alpha_j, && \forall\, j \in \c{A}, \\[0.2em] \big\|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\v\gamma)\, \v y\big\|_2 &\le \alpha_j, && \forall\, j \notin \c{A}. \end{aligned} $$

Moreover $\gamma_j = \|\v\theta_j\|_2 / \alpha_j$ for $j \in \c{A}$.

Compare with the original KKT conditions: the residual correlations $\m Z_j^\top(\v y - \m Z\widehat{\v\theta})$ have become $\m Z_j^\top \m G(\v\gamma)\v y$ — a single matrix–vector object shared by all groups. This is what will later let the MM algorithm update every group at once from one linear solve.

A new objective

Even better, these auxiliary conditions are themselves the KKT conditions of a clean convex problem. With $\v\alpha := [\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_J]^\top$ and $\odot$ the element-wise product, define

$$ g(\v\gamma) := \v y^\top \m G(\v\gamma)\, \v y + (\v\alpha \odot \v\alpha)^\top \v\gamma . $$

Lemma (Auxiliary Problem). The global minimizer $\widehat{\v\gamma} \in \argmin_{\v\gamma \in \bb{R}_+^J} g(\v\gamma)$ satisfies

$$ \begin{aligned} \big\|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\widehat{\v\gamma})\,\v y\big\|_2 &= \alpha_j, && \forall\, \widehat\gamma_j > 0, \\[0.2em] \big\|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\widehat{\v\gamma})\,\v y\big\|_2 &\le \alpha_j, && \forall\, \widehat\gamma_j = 0 . \end{aligned} $$

Corollary. The vector $\widehat{\v\theta} = \widehat{\m\Gamma}^2 \m Z^\top \m G(\widehat{\v\gamma})\,\v y$ (with $\widehat{\m\Gamma}$ the block-diagonal matrix carrying $\sqrt{\widehat\gamma_j}$) minimizes the original group lasso objective $f(\v\theta)$, and the activated set is exactly $\c{A} = \{j : \widehat\gamma_j > 0\}$.

So group selection is now encoded in the sign pattern of a non-negative vector in $\bb{R}^J$: $\gamma_j = 0$ means group $j$ is out, $\gamma_j > 0$ means it is in, and the coefficients are recovered by $\widehat{\v\theta}_j = \widehat\gamma_j \m Z_j^\top \m G(\widehat{\v\gamma})\,\v y$.

The catch: $g(\v\gamma)$ contains a matrix inverse, and naive coordinate descent on $\v\gamma$ would require an inner Newton iteration with a linear solve at every step — not competitive with BCD. What rescues the reparameterisation is a majorizing bound that separates the variables and updates them all simultaneously. That bound is the subject of Part 4.
