From lasso to group lasso

The lasso of Tibshirani (1996) performs estimation and variable selection simultaneously by penalising the $\ell_1$-norm of the regression coefficients: the non-smoothness of $|\cdot|$ at zero sets some coefficients exactly to zero. Often, however, predictors carry a natural group structure. In genomics, genes within the same pathway exhibit similar functions, and incorporating group structure has proven effective for biomarker identification — the use case that motivated the group lasso of Yuan & Lin (2006): select or discard entire groups of predictors at once.

Suppose we are given a dataset $(\v y, \m X)$ with response $\v y \in \bb{R}^n$ and feature matrix $\m X \in \bb{R}^{n \times p}$, decomposed into $J$ groups $\m X = [\m X_1, \ldots, \m X_J]$, $\m X_j \in \bb{R}^{n \times p_j}$, $p_1 + \cdots + p_J = p$. The linear model is

$$ \m Y = \beta_0 \m 1 + \m X_1 \v\beta_1 + \cdots + \m X_J \v\beta_J + \v\epsilon, \qquad \v\epsilon \sim \c{N}(\v 0, \sigma^2 \m I_n), $$

with predictors and response centred so the intercept can be dropped. Following the standardized group lasso of Simon & Tibshirani (2012), each block is sphered via its QR decomposition $\m X_j = \m Z_j \m R_j$, so that $\m Z_j^\top \m Z_j = \m I_{p_j}$. Writing $\v\theta_j := \m R_j \v\beta_j$, we minimize

$$ f(\v\theta) \;:=\; \frac{1}{2n}\Big\|\v y - \sum_{j=1}^{J} \m Z_j \v\theta_j\Big\|_2^2 \;+\; \lambda \sum_{j=1}^{J} \sqrt{p_j}\,\|\v\theta_j\|_2 . $$

The penalty $\sum_j \sqrt{p_j}\|\v\theta_j\|_2$ is non-smooth exactly where a whole group vanishes, so for each $j$ either $\widehat{\v\theta}_j = \v 0$ or every element of $\widehat{\v\theta}_j$ is non-zero. Larger $\lambda$ shrinks more groups to zero. With singleton groups ($p_j = 1$) this is precisely Tibshirani's lasso.

The KKT conditions

Set $\alpha_j := n\lambda\sqrt{p_j}$. The first-order (Karush–Kuhn–Tucker) condition for minimizing $f$ block-wise is

$$ \m Z_j^\top (\v r_{\neg j} - \m Z_j \v\theta_j) = \alpha_j\, \v g(\v\theta_j), \qquad \v r_{\neg j} := \v y - \sum_{i \ne j} \m Z_i \v\theta_i, $$

where $\v g$ is the subgradient of $\|\cdot\|_2$:

$$ \v g(\v\eta) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{\v\eta}{\|\v\eta\|_2}, & \v\eta \ne \v 0, \\[0.6em] \{\v s : \|\v s\|_2 \le 1\}, & \v\eta = \v 0 . \end{cases} $$

Writing $\c{A} := \{j : \widehat{\v\theta}_j \ne \v 0\}$ for the set of activated groups and $\m Z = [\m Z_1, \ldots, \m Z_J]$, the KKT conditions take the form we will use for the rest of the series:

$$ \begin{aligned} \m Z_j^\top (\v y - \m Z\widehat{\v\theta}) &= \frac{\widehat{\v\theta}_j}{\|\widehat{\v\theta}_j\|_2}\,\alpha_j, && \forall\, j \in \c{A}, \\[0.3em] \big\|\m Z_j^\top (\v y - \m Z\widehat{\v\theta})\big\|_2 &\le \alpha_j, && \forall\, j \notin \c{A}. \end{aligned} $$

An activated group's correlation with the residual sits exactly on the boundary $\alpha_j$; an unactivated group's sits inside it.

Block coordinate descent — and its limits

Because $\m Z_j^\top \m Z_j = \m I_{p_j}$, solving the KKT condition one block at a time has a closed form built from the lasso shrinkage operator

$$ S_{\alpha_j}(\v x) := \v x\,\big[1 - \alpha_j/\|\v x\|_2\big]_+, \qquad \v\theta_j \leftarrow S_{\alpha_j}\!\big(\m Z_j^\top \v r + \v\theta_j\big), $$

cycling $j = 1, \ldots, J$ — the block coordinate descent (BCD) algorithm of Breheny & Huang (2015), implemented in the R package grpreg. One cycle costs $\c{O}(np)$, which is cheap; the problem is the number of cycles. When $\lambda$ is small (many activated groups) or the feature matrix is poorly conditioned, BCD's iteration count can grow prohibitively.

In Part 3 we take a different route: a reparameterisation of the problem whose first-order conditions match the KKT conditions above, but which lives in $\bb{R}_+^J$ instead of $\bb{R}^p$ — the foundation of the MM algorithm.
