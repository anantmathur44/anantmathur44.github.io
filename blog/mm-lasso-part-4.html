Part 4: An Interesting Bound

MM Algorithms and the Lasso · Anant Mathur · July 2026

From Sedrakyan to matrices

To run MM on the auxiliary objective $g(\v\gamma) = \v y^\top \m G(\v\gamma)\,\v y + (\v\alpha \odot \v\alpha)^\top \v\gamma$ from Part 3, we need a surrogate that majorizes the awkward term $\v y^\top \m G(\v\gamma)\,\v y$. The tool is a matrix inequality used by Zhou, Hu, Zhou & Lange (2019) for variance-component models. Its scalar ancestor is Sedrakyan's inequality (a special case of Cauchy–Schwarz): for reals $\widetilde\sigma_j$ and positive reals $\sigma_j$,

$$ \frac{\big(\sum_{j} \widetilde\sigma_j\big)^2}{\sum_{j} \sigma_j} \;\le\; \sum_{j} \frac{\widetilde\sigma_j^2}{\sigma_j}. $$

The matrix version: for symmetric positive semi-definite $\m V_j$,

$$ \Big(\sum_{j} \sigma_j \m V_j\Big)^{-1} \;\preceq\; \Big(\sum_{j} \widetilde\sigma_j \m V_j\Big)^{-1} \Big(\sum_{j} \frac{\widetilde\sigma_j^2}{\sigma_j} \m V_j\Big) \Big(\sum_{j} \widetilde\sigma_j \m V_j\Big)^{-1}, $$

with equality when $\widetilde\sigma_j = \sigma_j$ — precisely the tangency an MM surrogate needs.

Applying it to $\m G(\v\gamma)$

Let $\v w = [w_1, \ldots, w_J]^\top$ be a probability vector ($w_j \ge 0$, $\sum_j w_j = 1$). Split the identity so every term is positive semi-definite — here orthogonality earns its keep, since $\m Z_j^\top \m Z_j = \m I_{p_j}$ makes $\m I - \m Z_j \m Z_j^\top$ a projection:

$$ \m G^{-1}(\v\gamma) = \sum_{j=1}^{J} (\gamma_j + w_j)\, \m Z_j \m Z_j^\top + \sum_{j=1}^{J} w_j \big(\m I - \m Z_j \m Z_j^\top\big). $$

Applying the matrix inequality with $\sigma_j = \gamma_j + w_j$, $\widetilde\sigma_j = \widetilde\gamma_j + w_j$ (and the projection terms held fixed), then sandwiching with $\v y^\top(\cdot)\v y$ and adding the linear penalty, yields the surrogate

$$ h_{\v w}(\v\gamma, \widetilde{\v\gamma}) = \sum_{j=1}^{J} \left[ \frac{(\widetilde\gamma_j + w_j)^2\, \|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\widetilde{\v\gamma})\,\v y\|_2^2}{\gamma_j + w_j} + w_j\Big(\|\m G(\widetilde{\v\gamma})\,\v y\|_2^2 - \|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\widetilde{\v\gamma})\,\v y\|_2^2\Big) + \alpha_j^2 \gamma_j \right]. $$

This is the "interesting bound". Two properties make it powerful. It is tangent: $h_{\v w}(\widetilde{\v\gamma}, \widetilde{\v\gamma}) = g(\widetilde{\v\gamma})$ and $g \le h_{\v w}$, so the MM descent property of Part 1 applies for any valid weights. And it is separable: the variables $\gamma_1, \ldots, \gamma_J$ decouple into $J$ one-dimensional problems, each solvable in closed form.

The closed-form update

Writing $\tau_j^{(t)} := \|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\v\gamma^{(t)})\,\v y\|_2$ and minimizing $h_{\v w}(\cdot, \v\gamma^{(t)})$ over $\gamma_j \ge 0$ gives, via the same lasso shrinkage operator $S$ from Part 2,

$$ \gamma_j^{(t+1)} \;\leftarrow\; S_{\alpha_j w_j}\!\Big( (w_j + \gamma_j^{(t)})\, \tau_j^{(t)} \Big) \Big/ \alpha_j, \qquad j = 1, \ldots, J . $$

All $J$ groups update concurrently from a single linear solve $\m G(\v\gamma^{(t)})\,\v y$, computed with a handful of conjugate-gradient steps that only touch activated groups. Note the role of the weights: when $w_j = 0$ the update degenerates to the multiplicative rule $\gamma_j^{(t+1)} = \gamma_j^{(t)} \tau_j^{(t)} / \alpha_j$, which can never move a group in or out of the activated set; only groups with $w_j > 0$ pass through the shrinkage operator and can switch status.

So which groups deserve positive weight? Only a total mass of 1 is available per iteration, and how it is allocated determines how fast the algorithm converges. The optimal allocation turns out to be a water-filling problem, solvable exactly in $\c{O}(J \ln J)$ — the subject of Part 5.
