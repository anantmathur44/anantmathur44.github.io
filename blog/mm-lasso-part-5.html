Deriving the weight problem

Part 4 ended with a question: with only a unit budget to distribute across the weights $w_1, \ldots, w_J$ at each MM iteration, where should it go? The principled answer is to pick the weights giving the least upper bound — the surrogate whose minimum drives $g$ down the most:

$$ \v w^{(t+1)} = \argmin_{\v w \,\in\, \bb{R}_+^J,\; \v 1^\top \v w = 1}\; \min_{\v\gamma \,\in\, \bb{R}_+^J}\; h_{\v w}(\v\gamma, \v\gamma^{(t)}). $$

The inner minimization is exactly the closed-form MM update from Part 4. Substituting it back into $h_{\v w}$ makes the bound separable in the weights: up to terms that do not depend on $\v w$, the problem becomes

$$ \widehat{\v w} = \argmin_{\v w \,\ge\, \v 0,\; \v 1^\top \v w = 1}\; \sum_{j=1}^{J} p_j(w_j), $$

a sum of one-dimensional convex costs. Writing $\widetilde\gamma_j$ for the current iterate, $\tau_j := \|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\widetilde{\v\gamma})\,\v y\|_2$ for the signal carried by group $j$, and defining the KKT gap

$$ \xi_j := \alpha_j - \tau_j, $$

each per-group cost has two branches, meeting smoothly at the knot $c_j := \widetilde\gamma_j \tau_j / \xi_j$:

$$ p_j(w_j) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{(\widetilde\gamma_j \tau_j)^2}{w_j} + 2\widetilde\gamma_j \tau_j^2, & w_j \ge c_j \text{ and } c_j \in (0, 1], \\[0.8em] 2\widetilde\gamma_j \alpha_j \tau_j - w_j\, \xi_j^2, & \text{otherwise.} \end{cases} $$

The branches have a precise meaning: giving group $j$ a weight $w_j \ge c_j$ is exactly enough for the shrinkage step to deactivate it ($\gamma_j \to 0$), landing on the nonlinear branch; smaller weights leave it active on the linear branch, where every unit of weight buys a constant $\xi_j^2$ of bound decrease. When $\xi_j < 0$ the knot is vacuous — no admissible weight can switch the group off, and $p_j$ stays linear on all of $[0, 1]$.

The KKT gap decides who gets weight

Why is $\xi_j = \alpha_j - \tau_j$ the right currency? Recall the auxiliary KKT conditions of Part 3: at the optimum, $\tau_j = \alpha_j$ for every active group and $\tau_j \le \alpha_j$ for every inactive one. So $\xi_j$ measures how far group $j$ currently is from optimality, and the cost derivation above says a unit of weight given to group $j$ initially removes $-p_j'(0) = \xi_j^2$ from the bound — the squared KKT gap is precisely the marginal value of weighting group $j$.

Now impose the KKT conditions of the weight problem itself. The simplex constraint contributes a multiplier $\nu^*$, and optimality requires

$$ -p_j'(\widehat w_j) = \nu^* \;\; \text{whenever } \widehat w_j > 0, \qquad \xi_j^2 \le \nu^* \;\; \text{whenever } \widehat w_j = 0 . $$

Reading this through the gap: an active group far from its KKT boundary (large $\xi_j^2$) is funded and pushed toward $\tau_j = \alpha_j$; an inactive group with $\xi_j < 0$ is violating its condition ($\tau_j > \alpha_j$ — it should be in the model), so it is funded and the shrinkage operator switches it on; an inactive group with $\xi_j \ge 0$ already satisfies its condition and gets $w_j = 0$. Every group still updates each iteration — the weights only decide who gets the privileged shrinkage treatment, much as greedy coordinate descent for the lasso picks the steepest coordinate (Wu & Lange, 2008).

A family of surrogates

The weight $w_j$ has a second life: it parameterizes a whole family of majorizers. To see it, restrict the auxiliary objective to a single group's coordinate $\gamma$ (groups mutually orthogonal, other coordinates held fixed). Along this slice, with $s := \|\m Z_j^\top \v y\|_2^2$,

$$ g(\gamma) = \frac{s}{1+\gamma} + \alpha^2 \gamma, \qquad h_w(\gamma, \widetilde\gamma) = \frac{s}{(1+\widetilde\gamma)^2} \left( \frac{(\widetilde\gamma + w)^2}{\gamma + w} + 1 - w \right) + \alpha^2 \gamma , $$

and every $w \in (0, 1]$ gives a valid surrogate: $h_w \ge g$ with tangency at $\gamma = \widetilde\gamma$. Minimizing $h_w$ recovers exactly the MM update of Part 4, $\gamma^+ = \big[(\widetilde\gamma + w)\,\widetilde\tau/\alpha - w\big]_+$ with $\widetilde\tau = \sqrt{s}/(1+\widetilde\gamma)$. Small $w$ gives a loose surrogate and a timid step; as $w \to 1$ the bound tightens onto $g$ — at $w = 1$ it is exact, and one MM step jumps straight to the slice minimizer. That is the whole tension: every group wants $w_j = 1$, but the weights share a unit budget. The weight problem resolves the competition, and $\xi_j^2$ is the exchange rate.

Part B
Water-filling
The exact solution of the weight problem, and two interactive views of it.

The solution is water-filling

Because each $-p_j'(w_j)$ starts at height $\xi_j^2$ and only decreases as $w_j$ grows, the KKT conditions above describe a classic water-filling scheme. Picture one vessel per group whose width at fill height $w_j$ is $-p_j'(w_j)$ — base width $\xi_j^2$, narrowing past the knot $c_j$. Pour the unit budget into whichever vessel is currently widest: funded surfaces then always share one common width — the water level $\nu$ — which only descends. When the budget is exhausted the level rests at $\nu^*$: vessels whose base $\xi_j^2$ never rose above the final level stay dry. Termination lands in one of two cases — either $\nu^*$ equals some group's base width $\xi_{(k)}^2$ (that pivot group takes the leftover budget), or $\nu^* = (\sum_{j \le k} \widetilde\gamma_j \tau_j)^2$ sits strictly between two base widths and the funded weights split proportionally to $\widetilde\gamma_j \tau_j$. Sorting the $\xi_j^2$ once gives the exact solution in $\c{O}(J \ln J)$ — negligible next to the linear solve in each MM step.

Setup & notation  —  \(j = 1, \dots, J\) indexes the groups

Group \(j\) holds \(p_j\) predictors \(\mathbf Z_j\); \(\tilde{\gamma}_j \ge 0\) is its coefficient scale at the current MM iterate \(\tilde{\boldsymbol\gamma}\). Two numbers summarise group \(j\) this iteration: \[ \alpha_j := n\lambda\sqrt{p_j} \;\;\text{(penalty threshold of group } j\text{)}, \qquad\quad \tau_j := \bigl\lVert \mathbf Z_j^{\top}\, \mathbf G(\tilde{\boldsymbol\gamma})\,\mathbf y \bigr\rVert \;\;\text{(signal carried by group } j\text{)}. \]

KKT conditions of the auxiliary problem

At the global minimiser \(\widehat{\boldsymbol\gamma}\), \[ \tau_j = \alpha_j \;\;\text{ for every active group } (\widehat\gamma_j > 0), \qquad\quad \tau_j \le \alpha_j \;\;\text{ for every inactive group } (\widehat\gamma_j = 0). \] The KKT gap \( \;\xi_j := \alpha_j - \tau_j\; \) measures how far group \(j\) is from optimality: \(\xi_j < 0\) means its signal exceeds the penalty threshold (the group “wants” weight); \(\xi_j \ge 0\) with \(\tilde\gamma_j = 0\) means group \(j\) already satisfies the conditions and is ignored by the allocation.

The weight problem  and  the deactivation threshold \(c_j\)

\[ \widehat{\boldsymbol w} \;=\; \mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}\limits_{\;\boldsymbol w \,\ge\, 0,\; w_1 + \cdots + w_J \,=\, 1}\;\; \sum_{j=1}^{J} p_j(w_j), \qquad\quad c_j := \frac{\tilde\gamma_j\,\tau_j}{\xi_j} \;\;\text{(smallest weight that switches group } j \text{ off)}. \] Giving group \(j\) weight \(w_j \ge c_j\) makes the next MM update set \(\gamma_j \to 0\); smaller weights leave it active. Note that when \(\xi_j < 0\) the threshold \(c_j < 0\) is vacuous — no weight can deactivate group \(j\), and \(p_j\) stays linear over the whole budget; more on this in the winner-takes-all remark below.

When no weight can deactivate a group. Deactivating group \(j\) requires \( w_j\,\xi_j \ge \tilde\gamma_j\tau_j \). So whenever \( \xi_j < \tilde\gamma_j\,\tau_j \) — equivalently \( c_j \notin (0,1] \), i.e. either \( c_j > 1 \) (the gap is positive but too small, the required weight exceeds the whole budget) or \( c_j < 0 \) (the gap is negative: group \(j\) violates the KKT conditions and no weight whatsoever can switch it off) — no admissible \( w_j \in [0,1] \) reaches the threshold, and \( p_j \) is linear on all of \([0,1]\) with constant slope \( -\xi_j^2 \). If this holds for every group \(j\), the objective \( \sum_j p_j(w_j) \) is linear over the simplex and the optimum sits at a vertex — winner takes all: the entire budget goes to the group with the largest \( \xi_j^2 \). This is the common situation mid-run; the “winner takes all” example below shows it with \( 0 < \xi_j < \tilde\gamma_j\tau_j \) for every group.

\(\nu^*\) — the final water level

The water level \( \nu := \max_j\, -p_j'(w_j) \) is the steepest slope still available; funded groups sit exactly on it, and it only descends. When the budget runs out the level rests at \( \nu^* \): funded groups have \( -p_j'(\widehat w_j) = \nu^* \), unfunded ones have \( \xi_j^2 \le \nu^* \). Where \( \nu^* \) lands decides the case:

The two termination cases  (groups sorted so \(\xi_{(1)}^2 \ge \xi_{(2)}^2 \ge \cdots\))

Case 1 — ν* equals a vessel’s base width ξ² \[ \nu^* = \xi_{(k)}^2 \quad\text{for some } k \] \[ \widehat w_{(j)} = \begin{cases} \tilde\gamma_{(j)}\tau_{(j)} / \sqrt{\nu^*}, & j < k \;\;\text{(nonlinear part)}\\[0.6ex] 1 - \sum_{i<k} \widehat w_{(i)}, & j = k \;\;\text{(pivot: leftover, in its base section)}\\[0.6ex] 0, & j > k \end{cases} \]
Case 2 — ν* strictly between two base widths \[ \xi_{(k)}^2 \;>\; \nu^* = \Bigl(\sum_{j \le k} \tilde\gamma_{(j)}\tau_{(j)}\Bigr)^{2} \;>\; \xi_{(k+1)}^2 \] \[ \widehat w_{(j)} = \begin{cases} \tilde\gamma_{(j)}\tau_{(j)} / \sqrt{\nu^*}, & j \le k \;\;\text{(all funded, nonlinear part)}\\[0.6ex] 0, & j > k \end{cases} \]
A split proportional to \(\tilde\gamma_j\tau_j\) among the funded groups.

Closing the loop

Assembling the pieces: the auxiliary reparameterisation (Part 3) turns group lasso into a convex problem over $\bb{R}_+^J$; the matrix bound (Part 4) gives a separable surrogate with a closed-form concurrent update; and the water-filling weight allocation (this part) aims each iteration at the groups furthest from optimality, at a negligible $\c{O}(J\ln J)$ overhead. The resulting MM algorithm converges globally to the group-lasso KKT point and, across simulations and real data, computes regularization paths up to an order of magnitude faster than block coordinate descent — with the largest gains where BCD struggles most: poorly conditioned features and many activated groups. Full theory, algorithms and experiments are in the paper.
