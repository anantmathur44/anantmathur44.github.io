Optimal weights

Part 4 ended with a question: with only a total mass of $1$ to distribute across the weights $w_1, \ldots, w_J$ at each MM iteration, where should it go? The principled answer is to pick the weights giving the least upper bound — the surrogate whose minimum drives $g$ down the most:

$$ \v w^{(t+1)} = \argmin_{\v w \,\in\, \bb{R}_+^J,\; \v 1^\top \v w = 1}\; \min_{\v\gamma \,\in\, \bb{R}_+^J}\; h_{\v w}(\v\gamma, \v\gamma^{(t)}). $$

This inner–outer problem looks daunting, but it is a constrained convex program with an exact solution computable in $\c{O}(J \ln J)$ time — the cost of sorting the array $[\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_J]$, where

$$ \xi_j := \big(\alpha_j - \tau_j^{(t)}\big)^2 = \Big(\alpha_j - \big\|\m Z_j^\top \m G(\v\gamma^{(t)})\,\v y\big\|_2\Big)^2 . $$

The quantity $\xi_j$ measures how far group $j$ is from satisfying the auxiliary KKT conditions of Part 3. The optimal allocation pours weight onto the groups with the largest $\xi_j$ — like pouring a fixed volume of water into a terrain whose depth profile is given by the KKT violations, filling the deepest basins first. Concretely: if $\xi_j$ is large and group $j$ is activated, weight is assigned to push it toward the KKT boundary; if $\gamma_j^{(t)} = 0$ but $\tau_j^{(t)} > \alpha_j$, the group should be active, and a positive $w_j$ lets the shrinkage operator switch it on; if $\gamma_j^{(t)} = 0$ and the KKT condition already holds, $w_j = 0$ and the group stays out. This mirrors greedy coordinate descent for the lasso (Wu & Lange, 2008) — but here every group still updates in the same iteration; the weights only decide which groups get the privileged shrinkage treatment.

How the water level works

The KKT conditions for the weight problem reveal a classic water-filling structure. Each group contributes a convex cost $p_j(w_j)$ whose negative slope $-p_j'(w_j)$ starts at height $\xi_j = (\alpha_j - \tau_j)^2$ and is non-increasing in $w_j$. Optimality requires a common level $\nu^*$ (the Lagrange multiplier of $\v 1^\top \v w = 1$) such that every group with positive weight has $-p_j'(\widehat w_j) = \nu^*$, while every group with $\xi_j < \nu^*$ receives $\widehat w_j = 0$. Sorting the $\xi_j$ in decreasing order and lowering the level until the unit mass is exhausted gives the exact solution — the getWeights algorithm — in $\c{O}(J \ln J)$ time. Groups whose KKT-violation columns rise above the water line get weight; the rest stay dry.

Closing the loop

Assembling the pieces: the auxiliary reparameterisation (Part 3) turns group lasso into a convex problem over $\bb{R}_+^J$; the matrix bound (Part 4) gives a separable surrogate with a closed-form concurrent update; and the water-filling weight allocation (this part) aims each iteration at the groups furthest from optimality, at a negligible $\c{O}(J\ln J)$ overhead. The resulting MM algorithm converges globally to the group-lasso KKT point and, across simulations and real data, computes regularization paths up to an order of magnitude faster than block coordinate descent — with the largest gains where BCD struggles most: poorly conditioned features and many activated groups. Full theory, algorithms and experiments are in the paper.
